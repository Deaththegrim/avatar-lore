Waterbending

Waterbending was first learned from the moon, which pushes and pulls the sea, and the Water Tribe still honours it as the art's teacher. Water is the element of change and its season is winter. A waterbender turns an attacker's strength back on him, shifting from a whip of water to a wall of ice to a cloud of mist in a breath. The art grows with the night and swells under the full moon; a waterbender caught in the open at noon, far from any river, is weaker than at dusk on a frozen shore. The best can draw water from the air or from living plants. The Northern Water Tribe keeps the art strong behind its walls of ice. In the South, raids over the decades took nearly every bender away, and a southern child who moves water is now a rare thing and a dangerous one to own.
