Buzzard wasps of the desert

Buzzard wasps nest in caves they dig into the rock spires of the Si Wong Desert: six-legged insects the size of a dog, with a vulture's bald head and hooked beak on a striped wasp's body, and razor-sharp talons. They eat carrion, dead travellers included, and hunt the living when hungry. Prey is carried back to the hive, chewed and turned into a honey-like paste on the walls that feeds the young and tastes of rotten meat. A colony attacks together when its nest is entered or when there is loud noise. Wasps circling overhead mean something below is dying. The desert would be a fouler place without them, and that is the only good thing anyone says of them.
